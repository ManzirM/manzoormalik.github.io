My Research

My research focuses on economic evaluation, population health, health inequalities, and the use of linked administrative data to evaluate household socio-economic support interventions and their impact on population wellbeing. I am particularly interested in using linked administrative data to evaluate household support interventions in natural policy experiment settings, with a focus on understanding their effects on health and health inequalities.

CHESS

CHESS is a research project led by the University of Liverpool that examines how local support services affect health and wellbeing. The study evaluates a range of Household Socio-economic Support Schemes (HSS), including financial assistance, employment and training, housing and debt advice, housing retrofit, and psychological and social support, to understand what works, for whom, and under what circumstances. The findings aim to inform the improvement of support services and health and well-being outcomes for disadvantaged households across the Liverpool City Region. The economic evaluation within CHESS examines whether Household Socio-economic Support Schemes improve mental health and wellbeing relative to the resources required to deliver them. The analysis considers both the costs of delivering these interventions and their associated health outcomes, generating evidence to support decision-making by service providers, policymakers, healthcare systems, local authorities, and community organisations. In addition, we are investigating methods for estimating health utility values using data from selected HSS cohorts linked to electronic health records (EHRs). This work aims to derive health utility estimates and identify potential EHR-based indicators that could serve as proxies for health status when preference-based measures of health-related quality of life are unavailable.

LOCDAS

This NIHR-funded project aims to improve the health of people with drug and alcohol dependencies in Liverpool by delivering multi-speciality long-term condition clinics within community drug and alcohol treatment services. The project evaluates whether providing integrated screening, diagnosis, and management of long-term conditions in community settings can improve access to healthcare, health outcomes and quality of life, while reducing health inequalities. It also examines healthcare utilisation and costs to assess the cost-effectiveness of this model of care and generate evidence to inform the future delivery of integrated health services for people experiencing drug and alcohol dependencies.

HUI

This project evaluates Cheshire East’s proactive management of High Intensity Users (HIUs) through multidisciplinary support aimed at reducing unplanned hospital use. Using linked healthcare data, we are assessing whether these schemes improve patient outcomes and reduce NHS healthcare utilisation and costs.
